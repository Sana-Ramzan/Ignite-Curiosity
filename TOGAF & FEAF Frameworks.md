🔷 TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework)
✅ Purpose:
Develop, manage, and govern enterprise architecture in any sector (global standard).

🧩 Core Elements:
ADM (Architecture Development Method) – Central methodology (9 phases)

Architecture Content Framework – Defines deliverables & artifacts

Enterprise Continuum – Repository of reusable assets

Reference Models – Technical and information integration guides

⚙️ ADM Phases (A–H + Requirements Mgmt):
A: Preliminary

B: Vision

C: Business Architecture

D: Info Systems Architecture (Data + App)

E: Tech Architecture

F: Solutions

G: Migration Planning

H: Governance

I: Change Management

🌟 Strengths:
Highly flexible

Suitable for private & public sectors

Emphasizes business-IT alignment

Includes templates & governance support

⚠️ Challenges:
Steep learning curve

Requires skilled professionals

May be complex for smaller orgs

🔷 FEAF (Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework)
✅ Purpose:
Align enterprise architecture to U.S. Federal goals – performance, transparency, shared services.

🧩 Core Elements:
Consolidated Reference Models (CRM):

PRM (Performance)

BRM (Business)

SRM (Service Components)

DRM (Data)

TRM (Technology)

SRM (Security)

Architecture Levels: Enterprise → Segment → Solution

Aligns with CPIC, OMB Policy, and budget planning

🌟 Strengths:
Ideal for government agencies

Links EA to investment and policy

Promotes standardization and inter-agency collaboration

⚠️ Challenges:
Less flexible than TOGAF

Geared toward U.S. federal context

Bureaucratic implementation

📊 TOGAF vs. FEAF Quick Comparison Table

Feature	TOGAF	FEAF
Origin	The Open Group	U.S. Federal Government
Use Case	Private + Public Sector	Federal Agencies & Contractors
Methodology	ADM + Customization	Reference Models + Compliance
Flexibility	High	Moderate
Focus	Architecture Process	Performance, Investment, Policy
Best For	Agile, Strategic Design	Policy Alignment, Budget Control
🧠 Strategic Recommendations:
TOGAF: Use for enterprise-wide digital transformation, global scalability, or innovation projects.

FEAF: Use for federal contracts, public sector programs, or when working within OMB compliance mandates.
